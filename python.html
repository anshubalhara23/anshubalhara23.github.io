Olist E-Commerce Python Analytics — Raw-Data Business Intelligence

In Progress

An independent, code-first analysis of the same Olist Brazilian e-commerce data behind my SQL warehouse project — but starting entirely from the 9 raw transactional CSVs rather than any already-cleaned tables. The goal is to demonstrate the full analyst workflow in Python: auditing raw data, cleaning and validating it, and turning it into structured business insight across five analytical areas.

Python Pandas NumPy Matplotlib RFM Segmentation Cohort Analysis

Rather than reusing the already-treated Gold-layer tables from OlistEcom, this project works from the 9 raw Olist CSVs (~1M+ combined records) independently, so the cleaning and validation decisions are made fresh in Python rather than inherited from the SQL pipeline.

Understand: profile every raw table (shape, dtypes, missing values, duplicates, primary keys) before touching anything.

Clean & validate: handle each dataset individually — duplicate detection, missing-value treatment, datetime standardization, and referential-integrity checks (e.g. confirming every order item maps to a valid order, product, and seller) before any merging happens.

Analyze: build five purpose-specific analytical modules:

  • Sales & Revenue — revenue trends, MoM growth, top/bottom categories and states
  • Customer Intelligence — repeat-purchase rate, RFM segmentation, cohort retention, Pareto analysis
  • Seller Intelligence — top sellers, satisfaction vs. revenue, seller performance matrix
  • Fulfillment Intelligence — delivery delays, late-delivery rate, impact on review scores
  • Product Intelligence — price distribution, freight burden, price vs. satisfaction

Connect: a cross-business analysis stage links delivery delays, freight cost, and seller performance to customer review scores, then translates the findings into concrete business recommendations rather than stopping at charts.

Built as a series of Jupyter notebooks (raw exploration → cleaning → validation → five analytical modules → cross-business analysis), using Pandas and NumPy for data handling and Matplotlib for the supporting visuals. One-to-many relationships between orders, order items, and payments are handled explicitly to avoid double-counting revenue when tables are joined — one of the main data-quality risks in this dataset.
